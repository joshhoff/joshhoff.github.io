Understanding the Use of "of" vs. "in"

Introduction

Welcome! At the C1 level, mastering prepositions like "of" and "in" is essential for fluency. These small words carry big meaning. This lesson will help you distinguish between subtle but important differences in how and when to use them.

Explanation

"Of" is often used to show:

  • Possession: The color of the sky
  • Composition: A piece of cake
  • Connection: The University of Oxford

"In" shows:

  • Location (physical or abstract): The keys are in the drawer.
  • Time periods: She was born in 1995.
  • Conditions: He's in trouble.

Pronouncing /əˈʧiv/ ("achieve") in American English

Introduction

Welcome to your pronunciation practice! This lesson focuses on mastering the American English pronunciation of words containing the /əˈʧiv/ sound—like "achieve," "retrieve," and "perceive." These words are key for academic and professional communication.

Explanation

  • /ə/ is the "schwa" sound, the most common vowel in English. It's unstressed and sounds like "uh".
  • /ʧ/ is the "ch" sound as in "chess."
  • So, achieve = /əˈʧiv/: uh-CHEEV.

Breakdown of "achieve":

  • /ə/ = unstressed "a"
  • /ʧ/ = "ch"
  • /iː/ = long "ee" vowel
  • /v/ = voiced "v"
