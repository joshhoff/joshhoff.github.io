Welcome to Your Advanced English Practice

As a Czech speaker learning English at the C1 level, you've already mastered many aspects of English. However, certain language patterns from Czech can still influence your English usage. This application focuses on common errors specific to Czech speakers and provides targeted practice to help you refine your English skills.

This application covers four key areas where Czech speakers often face challenges:

  • Articles (a/an/the) - which don't exist in Czech
  • Prepositions - which function differently in Czech
  • Word order - which is more flexible in Czech than in English
  • False friends - words that sound similar but have different meanings

Articles in English vs. Czech

One of the biggest challenges for Czech speakers is mastering English articles (a, an, the) because the Czech language doesn't have articles. In Czech, you might say Koupil jsem knihu (I bought book), while in English you must specify a book or the book.

Key Differences:

  • Definite article (the): Used when referring to something specific that both the speaker and listener know about.
  • Indefinite article (a/an): Used when introducing something for the first time or referring to any member of a group.
  • No article: Used with plural or uncountable nouns when speaking generally.

Common Errors for Czech Speakers:

I went to store yesterday.

I went to the store yesterday.

She is teacher.

She is a teacher.

I like the nature.

I like nature.

He gave me advice and the information.

He gave me advice and information.

Prepositions in English vs. Czech

Prepositions are particularly challenging because Czech and English use them differently. For example, in Czech you might say v pondělí (literally "in Monday") while in English we say on Monday.

Key Differences:

  • Time expressions: Czech and English use different prepositions for time (v pondělí → on Monday).
  • Location expressions: Spatial relationships are expressed differently (na stole → on the table).
  • Prepositional verbs: Many verbs require specific prepositions that differ between languages.

Common Errors for Czech Speakers:

I'll see you in Monday.

I'll see you on Monday.

I'm waiting on the bus.

I'm waiting for the bus.

She arrived to London yesterday.

She arrived in London yesterday.

I'm good in English.

I'm good at English.

Word Order in English vs. Czech

Czech has a relatively free word order due to its case system, while English relies heavily on word order to convey meaning. This difference often leads to errors when Czech speakers apply their native language patterns to English.

Key Differences:

  • Subject-Verb-Object (SVO): English strictly follows this pattern in statements, while Czech allows more flexibility.
  • Adverb placement: In Czech, adverbs can often be placed anywhere, but English has more specific rules.
  • Question formation: English uses auxiliary verbs and inverted word order for questions, unlike Czech.

Common Errors for Czech Speakers:

Yesterday went I to the store.

Yesterday I went to the store.

I like very much this book.

I like this book very much.

How long you have been waiting?

How long have you been waiting?

Always I drink coffee in the morning.

I always drink coffee in the morning.

False Friends: Czech and English

"False friends" are words that look or sound similar in both languages but have different meanings. These can cause confusion and misunderstandings for Czech speakers learning English.

Common Czech-English False Friends:

aktuální

Czech: current, present

English "actual": real, factual

sympatický

Czech: nice, likeable

English "sympathetic": showing compassion

eventuálně

Czech: possibly, perhaps

English "eventually": in the end, finally

kontrolovat

Czech: to check, verify

English "to control": to manage, direct

Common Errors for Czech Speakers:

The actual situation is difficult.

The current situation is difficult.

(When meaning "současná situace")

He's very sympathetic.

He's very nice/likeable.

(When meaning "sympatický")

I will eventually come to the party.

I will possibly come to the party.

(When meaning "eventuálně")

Please control your ticket.

Please check your ticket.

(When meaning "kontrolovat")
