Understanding Infinitives & Gerunds

🌍 Core Contrast: Spanish vs English

In Spanish, you often use just one infinitive form. In English, the choice between gerund (-ing) and infinitive (to + verb) depends on meaning, not just grammar rules.

En español: "Me gusta nadar" (infinitivo)
In English: "I like swimming" (gerundio - actividad general)
En español: "Decidí estudiar" (infinitivo)
In English: "I decided to study" (infinitivo - decisión en un momento)
⚠️ Important: Using the wrong form doesn't just break grammar rules—it can change the meaning completely!

🟢 Gerunds (-ing): General Activities & Experiences

Gerunds act like nouns and describe general activities, experiences, or ongoing states.

Common patterns with gerunds:
• like / love / hate / enjoy + -ing
• avoid / finish / keep / mind + -ing
• be interested in + -ing
• be good at + -ing
• spend time + -ing
Examples:
• She avoided talking about it.
• I'm interested in learning more.
• They enjoy cooking together.
• He's good at solving problems.

🔵 Infinitives (to + verb): Purpose & Future Intention

Infinitives express purpose, future intention, or decisions made at a specific moment.

Common patterns with infinitives:
• want / need / hope / plan + to + verb
• decide / refuse / agree + to + verb
• adjectives: important, easy, difficult, necessary + to + verb
Examples:
• We agreed to meet later.
• It's difficult to understand this rule.
• She wants to travel next year.
• They decided to start a business.

⚠️ "To" Is Not Always an Infinitive!

When "to" is a preposition (not part of an infinitive), it must be followed by a gerund (-ing).

Rule: If "to" follows a preposition or is part of a phrasal verb, use -ing
Examples:
• I look forward to seeing you. ✓
• I look forward to see you. ✗

• She's used to working late. ✓
• They object to paying extra fees. ✓

❌ Common Spanish-Influenced Errors

Error 1: "I enjoy to cook"
Correct: "I enjoy cooking"
Why: "Enjoy" describes a general activity/experience → use gerund
Error 2: "I'm interested to learn"
Correct: "I'm interested in learning"
Why: "In" is a preposition → must use gerund
Error 3: "I look forward to see you"
Correct: "I look forward to seeing you"
Why: "To" here is a preposition, not an infinitive marker
Error 4: "I stopped to smoke" (when meaning "quit")
Correct: "I stopped smoking"
Why: "Stop to smoke" means you paused to have a cigarette!

Practice Exercises

Verbs That Change Meaning

🛑 STOP

stop doing

Meaning: Quit an activity permanently or for a long time

Example: I stopped smoking last year. (I quit)

🕐 Past action that ended

stop to do

Meaning: Pause one action to do another

Example: I stopped to smoke a cigarette. (I paused my walk)

⏸️ Temporary pause for purpose

💭 REMEMBER

remember doing

Meaning: Have a memory of a past action

Example: I remember meeting her at the party. (memory)

📸 Looking back at the past

remember to do

Meaning: Not forget an obligation or task

Example: Remember to call your mom! (don't forget)

📋 Future obligation

🔬 TRY

try doing

Meaning: Experiment with something as a test

Example: Try adding more salt. (experiment)

🧪 Test/experiment

try to do

Meaning: Make an effort, attempt (may be difficult)

Example: I tried to open the door. (made effort)

💪 Effort/attempt

🎯 FORGET

forget doing

Meaning: Not remember a past action

Example: I'll never forget meeting you. (memory)

📸 Past memory

forget to do

Meaning: Not remember to do something (obligation)

Example: Don't forget to lock the door! (obligation)

📋 Future task

💡 Memory Tip:
• Gerund (-ing) = Looking back at the past or describing experiences
• Infinitive (to + verb) = Looking forward to the future or expressing purpose
