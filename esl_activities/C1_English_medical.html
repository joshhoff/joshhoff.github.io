📖 Quick Pronunciation Guide

The Letter "A" Sounds

  • /eɪ/ as in anemia, fatal
  • /æ/ as in cardiac, gastric
  • /ə/ as in hepatic, renal

The Letter "E" Sounds

  • /iː/ as in fever, seizure
  • /ɛ/ as in cerebral, septic
  • /ɪ/ as in benign, remission

💬 Conversation Practice

Practice speaking using these medical scenario prompts

Patient Consultation Advanced

Discussing Treatment Options

Scenario:

A patient has been diagnosed with chronic hypertension and is reluctant to start medication. Explain the condition, potential complications, and treatment options while addressing their concerns.

Key vocabulary to use:

hypertension /ˌhaɪpərˈtenʃən/ cardiovascular /ˌkɑːdioʊˈvæskjələr/ asymptomatic /ˌeɪsɪmptəˈmætɪk/

Discussion points:

  • • Explain what hypertension means and why it's called "the silent killer"
  • • Discuss lifestyle modifications vs. pharmacological intervention
  • • Address common misconceptions about blood pressure medication

Clinical Notes

Write a SOAP Note

Instructions:

A 54-year-old male patient presents with chest pain radiating to the left arm, shortness of breath, and diaphoresis. Write a SOAP note documenting this encounter. Include relevant medical terminology.

Required vocabulary (include pronunciation practice):

diaphoresis /ˌdaɪəfəˈriːsɪs/ angina /ænˈdʒaɪnə/ myocardial /ˌmaɪoʊˈkɑːrdiəl/ electrocardiogram /ɪˌlektroʊˈkɑːrdioʊˌɡræm/

💡 Writing Tips:

  • • Use precise medical terminology throughout
  • • Include patient history in the Subjective section
  • • Document vital signs and examination findings in Objective
  • • Provide differential diagnoses in Assessment
