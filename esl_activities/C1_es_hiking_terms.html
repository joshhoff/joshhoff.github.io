📚 Hiking Terms Library

🎒 Equipment & Gear
Base Weight
The weight of your backpack and all gear, excluding consumables like food, water, and fuel.
Ultralight
A hiking philosophy focused on carrying the lightest possible gear, typically with a base weight under 10 pounds.
Gaiters
Protective coverings worn over boots and lower legs to keep debris, water, and snow out of footwear.
Trekking Poles
Adjustable walking sticks that provide stability, reduce impact on joints, and can double as tent poles.
Stuff Sack
A lightweight bag used to compress and organize gear inside a backpack, often made of ripstop nylon.
Bivy
Short for bivouac sack; a minimalist, waterproof shelter that covers just a sleeping bag.
🧭 Navigation & Trail Terms
Switchback
A zigzag path up a steep slope that reduces the gradient and makes climbing easier.
Cairn
A stack of rocks used as a trail marker, especially in areas above treeline or where the path is unclear.
Blaze
A painted mark on trees or rocks indicating the trail route, often rectangular and colored.
Bushwhacking
Hiking through dense vegetation or off-trail terrain without a marked path.
Scree
Loose rock debris on a slope that can be challenging and dangerous to traverse.
Saddle
A low point along a ridge between two higher peaks, often used as a mountain pass.
💭 Feelings & Experiences
Trail Magic
Unexpected acts of kindness from strangers, like food, drinks, or rides offered to hikers.
Hiker Hunger
The intense appetite that develops after days of hiking and burning thousands of calories daily.
Trail Legs
The physical conditioning and muscle memory that develops after weeks of consistent hiking.
Bonking
Sudden fatigue and energy depletion caused by insufficient food or water intake.
Summit Fever
The dangerous obsession with reaching a peak despite deteriorating conditions or safety concerns.
Type 2 Fun
An experience that's miserable while happening but becomes a cherished memory afterward.

✍️ Written Activities

📝 Scenario Writing
Write a short story (150-200 words) about a challenging hiking situation. Use at least 5 advanced hiking terms from the library. Focus on describing the equipment needed, navigation challenges, and the hiker's emotional state.
🎒 Gear List Creation
Create a detailed gear list for a 5-day backpacking trip. Categorize items and explain your choices using proper hiking terminology. Consider base weight, weather conditions, and ultralight principles.
🗺️ Trail Description
Describe a challenging section of trail using advanced navigation and terrain terminology. Include elevation changes, trail markers, and potential hazards. Make it detailed enough for another hiker to visualize the route.
💭 Reflection Journal
Write a journal entry from the perspective of a long-distance hiker experiencing both physical and emotional challenges. Use terminology related to hiking feelings and experiences.
