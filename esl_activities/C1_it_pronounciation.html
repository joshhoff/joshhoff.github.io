Difficult Vowel Sounds for Italian Speakers

The /ɪ/ Sound /ɪ/
Italian Challenge: Italian doesn't have this short, relaxed "i" sound. Italians often replace it with /i/ (like "ee").
Tip: Make your tongue more relaxed and lower than for Italian "i". It's shorter and less tense.
🎯 Practice Tip:
Start with "ee" and relax your tongue slightly. The sound should be shorter and more central.
Practice Words:
bit sit ship fish quick think
The /æ/ Sound /æ/
Italian Challenge: This sound doesn't exist in Italian. Italians often use /a/ or /e/ instead.
Tip: Open your mouth wider than for Italian "a" and spread your lips slightly like smiling.
🎯 Practice Tip:
Say "ah" but pull the corners of your mouth back slightly. Think of it as "ah" + "eh" combined.
Practice Words:
cat hat back apple happy family
The /ʌ/ Sound /ʌ/
Italian Challenge: Italian speakers often use /a/ for this sound, making "cup" sound like "cap".
Tip: This is a central, relaxed vowel. Your tongue should be in the middle of your mouth, not low like /a/.
🎯 Practice Tip:
Say "ah" but move your tongue up and back slightly. It's like a lazy, unstressed "uh" sound.
Practice Words:
cup but love money mother country

Challenging Consonant Sounds

The /θ/ and /ð/ Sounds /θ/ /ð/
Italian Challenge: These "th" sounds don't exist in Italian. Speakers often substitute /s/, /z/, /t/, or /d/.
Tip: Put your tongue tip between your teeth. /θ/ is voiceless (think), /ð/ is voiced (this).
🎯 Practice Tip:
Start by exaggerating - stick your tongue out slightly between your teeth, then gradually pull it back.
/θ/ Words (voiceless):
think three thank month

/ð/ Words (voiced):
this that mother weather
The /w/ Sound /w/
Italian Challenge: Italian speakers often substitute /v/ for /w/, making "wine" sound like "vine".
Tip: Round your lips like you're going to whistle, then quickly move to the next vowel. Don't touch your teeth with your lip.
🎯 Practice Tip:
Practice "oo-ah" quickly, then try "wah". Your lips should be rounded, not touching your teeth.
Practice Words:
water wine work woman away always
The /ŋ/ Sound /ŋ/
Italian Challenge: Italian speakers often add /g/ after /n/ in "-ing" endings, saying "sing-guh" instead of "sing".
Tip: The back of your tongue touches your soft palate. Don't release with a /g/ sound.
🎯 Practice Tip:
Say "sing" and hold the final sound. Feel where your tongue is - that's the /ŋ/ position.
Practice Words:
sing running working thinking morning evening
