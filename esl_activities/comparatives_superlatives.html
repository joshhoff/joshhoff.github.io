Understanding Comparative & Superlative Adjectives

What are they?

We use comparative adjectives to compare two things. We use superlative adjectives to compare one thing with all others in a group.

Comparative Rules

  • • Short adjectives (1 syllable): add -er
    tall → taller, old → older
  • • Short adjectives ending in -e: add -r
    nice → nicer, large → larger
  • • Short adjectives ending in consonant + vowel + consonant: double the final consonant and add -er
    big → bigger, hot → hotter
  • • Two-syllable adjectives ending in -y: change y to i and add -er
    happy → happier, easy → easier
  • • Longer adjectives (2+ syllables): use more before the adjective
    beautiful → more beautiful, expensive → more expensive

Superlative Rules

  • • Short adjectives (1 syllable): add -est
    tall → tallest, old → oldest
  • • Short adjectives ending in -e: add -st
    nice → nicest, large → largest
  • • Short adjectives ending in consonant + vowel + consonant: double the final consonant and add -est
    big → biggest, hot → hottest
  • • Two-syllable adjectives ending in -y: change y to i and add -est
    happy → happiest, easy → easiest
  • • Longer adjectives (2+ syllables): use the most before the adjective
    beautiful → the most beautiful, expensive → the most expensive

Irregular Forms

Adjective Comparative Superlative
good better the best
bad worse the worst
far farther/further the farthest/furthest
little less the least

Usage Tips

  • • Use "than" with comparatives: She is taller than her brother.
  • • Use "the" before superlatives: He is the tallest in his class.
  • • You can use "much", "even", or "a lot" to emphasize comparatives: This book is much better than the other one.
