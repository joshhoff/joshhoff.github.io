Guess Who, Where & What?

A fun speaking game with relative clauses for ESL students

What is this game?

This is a speaking game for A2 level English students to practice using relative clauses while having fun with classmates!

Relative clauses help us give more information about people, places, and things using relative pronouns:

who which that where whose whom

Person

Choose a famous person that your classmates might know.

Place

Choose an interesting place in the world.

Activity

Choose a fun or common activity.

How to Play

1

Form pairs

Work with a partner to choose:

  • A person (real or fictional)
  • A place (city, country, or specific location)
  • An activity (sport, hobby, etc.)
2

Create clues with relative clauses

Make 2-3 sentences about each item using relative clauses with who, which, that, where, whose, or whom.

3

Challenge other pairs

Take turns sharing your clues with other pairs in the class. They must guess what your person, place, or activity is.

4

Score points

If a team guesses correctly within two tries, they get a point!

Relative Pronouns Guide

WHO

Used for people

Example: The woman who lives next door is a doctor.

WHICH

Used for things and animals

Example: The book which I bought yesterday is interesting.

THAT

Used for people or things

Example: The movie that we watched was exciting.

WHERE

Used for places

Example: The restaurant where we had dinner was excellent.

WHOSE

Used to show possession

Example: The boy whose bicycle was stolen is my neighbor.

WHOM

Used for people as objects (formal)

Example: The professor whom I admire most teaches history.

Examples of Clues

Person Example

Clues:

  • This is a person who founded a major tech company.
  • He is someone whose products are used by billions of people.
  • He is the entrepreneur whom many young business students admire.

Answer: Steve Jobs

Place Example

Clues:

  • This is a place where ancient gladiators once fought.
  • It's a structure which was built almost 2000 years ago.
  • It's a famous landmark that attracts millions of tourists every year.

Answer: The Colosseum

Activity Example

Clues:

  • This is an activity that involves creating images on paper or canvas.
  • It's a form of expression which has existed since prehistoric times.
  • It's a skill whose masters include Leonardo da Vinci and Picasso.

Answer: Painting

More Challenging Examples

Challenging Example 1

Clues:

  • This is a person who developed a theory that changed our understanding of the universe.
  • He was a scientist whose equation E=mc² is known worldwide.
  • He was someone whom the Nobel committee recognized for his work on the photoelectric effect, not relativity.

Answer: Albert Einstein

Challenging Example 2

Clues:

  • This is a place where you can find the world's largest collection of mummies.
  • It's a building which houses artifacts that are over 4,000 years old.
  • It's an institution whose most famous exhibit is the Rosetta Stone.

Answer: The British Museum

Tips for Teachers

Before the Game

  • Review all relative pronouns with students
  • Practice forming different types of relative clauses
  • Provide vocabulary that might be useful
  • Show examples of good clues using various pronouns
  • Explain the difference between defining and non-defining relative clauses

During the Game

  • Monitor pairs to ensure they're using relative clauses correctly
  • Encourage students to use a variety of relative pronouns
  • Award bonus points for using more complex structures
  • Use the timer to keep the game moving at a good pace
  • Keep track of points on the scoreboard
