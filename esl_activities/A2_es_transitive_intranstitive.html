🎓 Understanding Transitive & Intransitive Verbs

Transitive verbs need a direct object to complete their meaning.

Intransitive verbs don't need a direct object - they can stand alone!

🌎 For Spanish Speakers

Good news! Spanish has the same concept, but English is more strict about it.

In Spanish: 'Yo leo' (I read) - the object can be implied

In English: 'I read' sounds incomplete - we need 'I read a book'

Tip: If you can ask '¿qué?' or '¿a quién?' after the verb, it's probably transitive!

🎯 Transitive Verbs

Pattern: Subject + Verb + Object

  • I read a book (Leo un libro)
  • She bought groceries (Ella compró comestibles)
  • They watched a movie (Ellos vieron una película)
  • He loves pizza (Él ama la pizza)

🌟 Intransitive Verbs

Pattern: Subject + Verb

  • The baby sleeps (El bebé duerme)
  • Birds fly (Los pájaros vuelan)
  • We arrived early (Llegamos temprano)
  • The sun shines (El sol brilla)

💡 Quick Tip!

Ask yourself: "What?" or "Who?" after the verb. If there's an answer, it's transitive!

Example: "She bought..." → "She bought what?" → "groceries" (transitive)

⚠️ Common Mistakes for Spanish Speakers

❌ Wrong: "I like" (incomplete)

✅ Correct: "I like pizza" (complete)

In Spanish: "Me gusta" is complete, but English needs the object!

❌ Wrong: "She is eating" (when you mean she's eating something specific)

✅ Better: "She is eating lunch" (more natural)

Spanish: "Está comiendo" is fine, but English often wants the object!
