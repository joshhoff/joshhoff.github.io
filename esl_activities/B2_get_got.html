Welcome to "Get" vs. "Got"

"Get" and "got" are among the most versatile and commonly used verbs in English. They can be confusing because they have many different meanings and uses.

In this lesson, you'll learn:

  • The basic forms of "get" and when to use each one
  • The many meanings and uses of "get" and "got"
  • Common phrases and idioms with "get"
  • How to use "get" and "got" in everyday conversations

Did you know?

"Get" is one of the top 10 most frequently used verbs in English! It has over 20 different meanings and can function as both a main verb and an auxiliary verb.

Understanding "Get" and "Got"

Basic Forms

Form Example When to Use
get (base form) I usually get up at 7 AM. Present simple, infinitive
gets (3rd person) She gets nervous before exams. Present simple (he/she/it)
getting (present participle) I'm getting better at English. Continuous tenses, gerund
got (past simple) I got a new job last month. Past simple
gotten (past participle - US) I've gotten used to the weather here. Perfect tenses (American English)
got (past participle - UK) I've got used to the weather here. Perfect tenses (British English)

Main Uses of "Get" and "Got"

1. Receive or obtain

I got a gift from my friend.

She gets a lot of emails every day.

2. Buy or purchase

I need to get some groceries.

He got a new car last week.

3. Arrive or reach

What time did you get home?

We got to the airport early.

4. Become

It's getting dark outside.

She got angry when I was late.

5. Understand

I don't get this math problem.

She finally got the joke.

6. Possession (have)

I've got three brothers.

Special Note: "Have got" vs. "Have"

In British English especially, "have got" is commonly used instead of "have" to express possession:

  • "I've got a car" = "I have a car"
  • "She's got blue eyes" = "She has blue eyes"
  • "Have you got time?" = "Do you have time?"

Remember: "Have got" is only used in present tense. For past tense, use "had":
"I had a car" (not "I had got a car")

Common Expressions with "Get" and "Got"

Phrasal Verbs with "Get"

get up

Meaning: to rise from bed; to stand up

I get up at 7 AM every morning.

She got up from her chair to greet the guests.

get on/along with

Meaning: to have a good relationship with

I get on well with my colleagues.

Do you get along with your neighbors?

get over

Meaning: to recover from (illness/disappointment)

It took me weeks to get over the flu.

She hasn't gotten over her breakup yet.

get back

Meaning: to return

What time did you get back from work?

I'll get back to you with an answer tomorrow.

get away

Meaning: to escape; to go on vacation

The thief got away before the police arrived.

We're planning to get away for the weekend.

get rid of

Meaning: to dispose of; to eliminate

I need to get rid of these old clothes.

How can I get rid of this headache?

Common Expressions and Idioms

get the hang of something

It took me a while to get the hang of driving on the left side of the road.

(to understand how to do something)

get on someone's nerves

His constant humming really gets on my nerves.

(to annoy someone)

get cold feet

She got cold feet before her presentation and decided not to do it.

(to become nervous about doing something)

get the point

I don't think he got the point of my argument.

(to understand the meaning)

Conversation Practice

Dialogue Practice

Read the following conversation that uses "get" and "got" in different ways:

A

Hi Sarah! How's it going? I got your message about the party.

Hey Mike! I'm good, thanks. I'm just trying to get everything ready for tonight. Have you got time to help me?

B
A

Sure! I'll get there around 5 PM. What do you need me to bring? I can get some drinks on my way.

That would be great! I've already got the food, but we're getting low on drinks. By the way, did you get the email about our project deadline?

B
A

Yes, I got it yesterday. I'm getting a bit worried about finishing on time. I haven't got much done yet.

Don't worry, we'll get it done. Let's get together on Monday to work on it. I think we'll get better results if we collaborate.

B

Analysis of "Get" and "Got" in the Dialogue

  • "I got your message" - received
  • "trying to get everything ready" - prepare
  • "Have you got time" - possess/have
  • "I'll get there" - arrive
  • "I can get some drinks" - buy/obtain
  • "I've already got the food" - possess/have
  • "we're getting low on drinks" - becoming
  • "did you get the email" - receive
  • "I'm getting worried" - becoming
  • "I haven't got much done" - completed
  • "we'll get it done" - complete
  • "Let's get together" - meet
  • "we'll get better results" - obtain/achieve
