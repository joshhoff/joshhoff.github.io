Countable & Uncountable Nouns

Master the fundamentals of English grammar

📚 The Rules

🔢 Countable Nouns

Nouns you can count individually. They have singular and plural forms.

  • Can use: a, an, one, two, three...
  • Can use: many, few, several
  • Examples: book/books, apple/apples

💧 Uncountable Nouns

Nouns representing substances, concepts, or masses. No plural form.

  • Cannot use: a, an, one, two...
  • Can use: much, little, some
  • Examples: water, advice, furniture

Common Quantifiers

Many / Much

Many books (countable)
Much water (uncountable)

A Few / A Little

A few students (countable)
A little time (uncountable)

Some / Any

Works with both!
Some chairs, some milk

Most

Works with both!
Most people, most information

💡 Examples in Context

Countable with "many": "Many tourists visit the museum every day. I saw three tourists taking photos near the fountain."

Countable with "a few": "A few tourists asked me for directions to the train station. Only a few tourists speak the local language fluently."

Countable with "most": "Most tourists prefer to visit during the summer months. A tourist left their camera at the restaurant, and most tourists enjoy trying the local cuisine."

Mixed with uncountable: "Many tourists (countable) need a little information (uncountable) about the city. Most tourists (countable) don't have much time (uncountable) to explore everything."
