Airport Phrases

Easy

Where is the check-in counter?

Gdzie jest stanowisko odprawy?

Pronunciation tip:

Focus on the "wh" sound in "where" - say it as /wer/ not /ver/

Practice: Where - /wer/

Medium

I need to check this luggage through to my final destination.

Muszę nadać ten bagaż do miejsca docelowego.

Pronunciation tip:

The "th" in "through" is challenging - place your tongue between your teeth and blow air out.

Practice: through - /θruː/

Hard

My flight has been delayed. Is there another available flight?

Mój lot został opóźniony. Czy jest dostępny inny lot?

Pronunciation tip:

The "th" in "another" is voiced - vibrate your vocal cords while your tongue is between your teeth.

Practice: another - /əˈnʌðər/

Medium

Which terminal do I need for international flights?

Który terminal jest dla lotów międzynarodowych?

Pronunciation tip:

Focus on the "w" sound in "which" - say it as /w/ not /v/

Practice: which - /wɪtʃ/

Hotel Phrases

Easy

I have a reservation under the name Smith.

Mam rezerwację na nazwisko Smith.

Pronunciation tip:

The "th" in "Smith" is unvoiced - place your tongue between your teeth without vibrating your vocal cords.

Practice: Smith - /smɪθ/

Medium

Could you recommend a good restaurant nearby?

Czy mógłby Pan/Pani polecić dobrą restaurację w pobliżu?

Pronunciation tip:

The "r" in "restaurant" is different from Polish "r" - it's less rolled.

Practice: restaurant - /ˈrestərɒnt/

Hard

The air conditioning in my room isn't working properly.

Klimatyzacja w moim pokoju nie działa prawidłowo.

Pronunciation tip:

Focus on the "th" sound in "the" - it's voiced (vibrate your vocal cords).

Practice: the - /ðə/

Medium

What time is the breakfast served?

O której godzinie jest serwowane śniadanie?

Pronunciation tip:

The "r" in "breakfast" is silent - focus on saying /ˈbrekfəst/

Practice: breakfast - /ˈbrekfəst/

Restaurant Phrases

Easy

A table for two, please.

Stolik dla dwóch osób, poproszę.

Pronunciation tip:

The "th" in "three" is unvoiced - place your tongue between your teeth.

Practice: three - /θriː/

Medium

I'm allergic to shellfish. Does this dish contain any?

Jestem uczulony na owoce morza. Czy to danie zawiera jakieś?

Pronunciation tip:

The "sh" in "shellfish" is pronounced /ʃ/ - push air through your teeth while making a "shh" sound.

Practice: shellfish - /ˈʃelfɪʃ/

Hard

Could I have the bill, please? We're in a hurry.

Czy mogę prosić o rachunek? Spieszymy się.

Pronunciation tip:

The "h" in "hurry" is pronounced - don't skip it as in Polish.

Practice: hurry - /ˈhʌri/

Medium

I would like my steak medium-rare, please.

Poproszę stek średnio wysmażony.

Pronunciation tip:

The "r" in "rare" is different from Polish "r" - it's less rolled.

Practice: rare - /reər/

Common Pronunciation Challenges for Polish Speakers

The "th" Sound

Polish doesn't have the "th" sound, making it challenging for Polish speakers.

Practice with:

Unvoiced "th": think, three, thank

Voiced "th": the, this, that

The "w" vs "v" Sound

In Polish, the letter "w" is pronounced like "v" in English.

Practice with:

"w" sound: we, what, where

"v" sound: very, vacation, visit

The "r" Sound

The Polish "r" is rolled/trilled, while the English "r" is not.

Practice with:

English "r": red, right, around

The "h" Sound

The "h" in Polish is often silent or very soft.

Practice with:

English "h": hello, help, hotel
