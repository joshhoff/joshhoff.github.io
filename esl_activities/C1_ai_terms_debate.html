Key AI Debate Vocabulary

Machine Learning

A subset of AI that enables systems to learn and improve from experience without being explicitly programmed.

Neural Network

Computing systems inspired by biological neural networks that form the basis of deep learning algorithms.

Algorithm

A set of rules or instructions given to an AI system to help it learn, reason, and make decisions.

Natural Language Processing

The ability of computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language.

Computer Vision

The field of AI that enables machines to derive meaningful information from digital images and videos.

Generative AI

AI systems that can create new content, such as text, images, or music, based on what they've learned.

Algorithmic Bias

When an AI system reflects or amplifies existing prejudices in its training data or design.

Transparency

The degree to which AI systems' operations and decision-making processes are visible and understandable.

Accountability

The principle that developers and deployers of AI should be responsible for its actions and impacts.

Explainability

The ability to explain how and why an AI system reached a particular conclusion or decision.

Autonomy

The degree to which AI systems can operate and make decisions independently of human control.

Digital Divide

The gap between those who have access to AI technologies and those who do not.

Automation

The use of AI to perform tasks previously done by humans, often affecting employment patterns.

Technological Unemployment

Job loss caused by technological change, particularly automation through AI.

Surveillance Capitalism

Economic system centered around the commodification of personal data for profit through prediction and modification of behavior.

Technological Singularity

A hypothetical point where AI surpasses human intelligence, potentially leading to rapid technological growth.

Augmented Intelligence

The concept that AI should complement human intelligence rather than replace it.

Digital Citizenship

The responsible use of technology and participation in digital society in the age of AI.

Debate Structure & Connecting Words

Formal Debate Structure

1. Opening Statement

Introduce your position clearly and provide a brief overview of your main arguments.

2. Main Arguments

Present 2-3 key points with supporting evidence and examples.

3. Rebuttal

Address and counter the opposing side's arguments.

4. Conclusion

Summarize your position and main points, emphasizing why your argument is stronger.
