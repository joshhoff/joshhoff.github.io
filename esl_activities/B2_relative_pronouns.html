Relative Pronouns

B2 Level Grammar

Understanding Relative Pronouns

Relative pronouns connect clauses and provide additional information about a noun. At B2 level, you'll master nuanced usage including non-defining clauses, formal preposition placement, and reduced relative clauses—all essential for academic and professional English.

👤

WHO / WHOM

Use for people only. "Who" is the subject; "whom" is the object (especially after prepositions in formal English).

Subject (Who)

The architect who designed this won an award.

The architect does the designing—"who" is the subject.

Object (Whom)

The colleague to whom I reported is retiring.

I reported to the colleague—"whom" is the object of the preposition "to".

💡 Tip: Test by replacing with he/she (who) or him/her (whom)

Example: "I reported to him" → use whom

📌

WHICH

Use for things, animals, and occasionally entire clauses. Common in both defining and non-defining clauses.

Defining Clause (No Commas)

The proposal which was submitted was rejected.

Specifies which proposal—essential information. In casual English, "that" is also acceptable.

Non-Defining Clause (With Commas)

He passed all exams, which surprised everyone.

Adds extra information about the entire previous clause. "Which" is required here.

💡 Key B2 Rule: Never use "that" in non-defining clauses or after prepositions

🔗

WHOSE

Indicates possession. Works for people, things, and organizations. This is often overlooked but very common at B2+.

People

The student whose essay won is presenting today.

The essay belongs to the student.

Things/Organizations

The company whose profits doubled is expanding rapidly.

The profits belong to the company. This usage is perfectly acceptable in B2.

Non-Defining Context

Dr. Chen, whose research is groundbreaking, joined last year.

Extra information about Dr. Chen—requires commas and always uses "whose", never "that".

🗺️

WHERE / WHEN / WHY

Relative adverbs that replace prepositional phrases: "in which," "at which," "for which."

Where (Place)

The city where she grew up is peaceful.

Alternative (formal): "The city in which she grew up..."

"Where" is more natural and commonly used in speech and writing.

When (Time)

The 1920s was the decade when jazz exploded.

Alternative (formal): "The decade during which jazz exploded..."

Why (Reason)

The reason why she quit is unclear.

Alternative (formal): "The reason for which she quit..."

Note: "Why" typically follows the word "reason"; "the why" alone is informal.

💡 B2 Insight: Relative adverbs are simpler and more natural than their formal "preposition + which" equivalents

🎯 B2 Key Concepts

Defining vs. Non-Defining Clauses

Non-defining clauses (with commas) add extra info. You cannot use "that" in non-defining clauses.

✓ Correct

My sister, who lives in Berlin, is visiting.

✗ Incorrect

My sister, that lives in Berlin, is visiting.

Preposition + Relative Pronoun (Formal)

In formal English, prepositions come before the pronoun. Use whom for people and which for things.

Formal: The issue about which we spoke is resolved.

Informal: The issue which we spoke about is resolved.

Reduced Relative Clauses

Active clauses reduce to present participle; passive clauses reduce to past participle.

Full: People who live near airports…

Reduced: People living near airports…

Full: The report which was published…

Reduced: The report published…

Speaking Practice Prompts

Use these prompts to practice speaking naturally with relative pronouns. Record yourself or practice with a partner. Click on each card to see a sample response using B2-level relative pronouns.

🎯 Describe a book or film

Tell me about a book or film that moved you. Use relative clauses to explain what made it special.

Sample Response:

"The novel which I read last month was absolutely transformative. It's about a woman whose life completely changes after meeting someone who challenges everything she believes. The themes which the author explores—identity, belonging, resilience—really resonated with me."

🌍 Explain your ideal destination

Describe a place you dream of visiting. Explain what attracts you and why using relative pronouns.

Sample Response:

"I'd love to visit Japan, a country where ancient traditions and cutting-edge technology coexist. The temples, which date back centuries, fascinate me, and I'm also drawn to the modern cities whose energy is legendary. I want to visit during spring, when the cherry blossoms are in bloom."

👨‍💼 Talk about a mentor or role model

Describe someone who has influenced you. Explain the qualities they possess and the impact they've had.

Sample Response:

"My mentor is someone whose integrity and vision have shaped who I am today. She's a woman who rose from humble beginnings to lead her industry, always remembering the people whose support got her there. The advice which she gave me when I doubted myself was invaluable."

🎓 Discuss a skill you're learning

What skill are you developing? Explain your motivation and describe the challenges and progress.

Sample Response:

"I'm currently learning photography, a hobby which I started during the pandemic. The techniques that I'm mastering now are much more complex than those which I learned initially. There's a community online whose feedback has been instrumental in my development."

🌱 Share a turning point in your life

Describe a moment that changed your perspective or direction. Explain the circumstances and consequences.

Sample Response:

"There was a moment in university when I realized that the career path I was pursuing didn't align with my values. The professor whose class prompted this reflection encouraged me to follow my passion rather than security. It was a decision for which I'm eternally grateful, even though it was frightening at the time."

🌐 Comment on a social or political issue

Discuss a current issue that concerns you. Share the reasons why it matters and what solutions you propose.

Sample Response:

"Climate change is an issue which demands urgent action from governments whose policies shape our future. The communities where environmental degradation is most severe are often those whose voices are least heard. We need solutions that address both environmental and social justice."
