🤔 Simple Present Questions

Learn to ask questions in English!

🎯 What are Simple Present Questions?

Simple present questions help us ask about things that happen regularly, facts, or habits. We use them every day!

Formula: Do/Does + Subject + Base Verb + ?

📋 The Rules:

With I, You, We, They

Use "DO"

✅ Do you like pizza?

✅ Do they play soccer?

With He, She, It

Use "DOES"

✅ Does she speak English?

✅ Does it work?

🔄 Statement → Question

Statement: You like music. → Question: Do you like music?

Statement: She works here. → Question: Does she work here?

Notice: The main verb becomes the base form (work, not works) in questions!

🌟 Common Question Words:

What do you eat for breakfast?

Where does he live?

When do they arrive?

Why does she study English?

Activity 1: Choose the Correct Question

Transform this statement: "She plays tennis."

Activity 2: Pick the Right Helper

_____ you speak Spanish?

Activity 3: Complete the Question

_____ your brother _____ in a bank?

Activity 4: Question Word Practice

_____ does she live? (asking about location)

Activity 5: Fill in the Blanks

Write the correct form:

_____ they _____ coffee every morning?

Activity 6: Transform the Sentence

Change to a question: "It rains a lot here."
🗣️ Daily Routine Questions

Practice asking about daily activities:

  • Do you wake up early?
  • What time do you eat breakfast?
  • Does your family watch TV together?
  • Where do you usually have lunch?
🎯 Hobby & Interest Questions

Ask about hobbies and interests:

  • Do you play any sports?
  • What kind of music does your friend like?
  • Does your teacher speak other languages?
  • Do you read books in your free time?
🏠 Family & Friends Questions

Learn about people around you:

  • Does your mother work?
  • Where do your grandparents live?
  • Do your friends study English too?
  • What does your father do for work?
🎲 Practice Challenge

Try this with a partner:

Take turns asking and answering these questions. Remember to use complete sentences in your answers!

Example: "Do you like pizza?" → "Yes, I do. I like pizza very much!" or "No, I don't. I prefer pasta."
