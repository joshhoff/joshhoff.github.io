W vs V Pronunciation in English

For Czech speakers, distinguishing between the English "W" and "V" sounds can be challenging since Czech only has the "V" sound. Let's practice the difference!

How to Pronounce "W"

To pronounce "W":

  1. Round your lips as if you're going to whistle or say "oo"
  2. Keep your lips rounded but slightly open
  3. Your lips should not touch your teeth
  4. Push air out gently without vibrating your vocal cords

Try saying:

  • Water (not Vater)
  • Wait (not Vait)
  • Wine (not Vine)
  • Walk (not Valk)

How to Pronounce "V"

To pronounce "V":

  1. Place your upper teeth on your lower lip
  2. Push air out while vibrating your vocal cords
  3. You should feel vibration in your lips
  4. This is similar to the Czech "v" sound

Try saying:

  • Very (not Wery)
  • Vacation (not Wacation)
  • Village (not Willage)
  • Visit (not Wisit)

Common Mistake for Czech Speakers

In Czech, the letter "w" is pronounced as "v" (like in "Volkswagen"). In English, "w" and "v" are different sounds. Practice saying: "We visited the wonderful village with a very wide view."

Common Travel Phrases

Asking for Directions

  • "Excuse me, could you tell me how to get to...?"
  • "Is it far from here?"
  • "Could you show me on the map, please?"
  • "Is there public transportation nearby?"

At Restaurants

  • "Could I see the menu, please?"
  • "What do you recommend?"
  • "I have a food allergy to..."
  • "Could we have the bill, please?"

Accommodation

  • "I have a reservation under the name..."
  • "Is breakfast included?"
  • "What time is check-out?"
  • "Could I have an extra pillow, please?"

Transportation

  • "When is the next train/bus to...?"
  • "I'd like a one-way/return ticket to..."
  • "Does this bus stop at...?"
  • "Could you call a taxi for me, please?"

Modal Verbs in English

Modal verbs are special verbs that express necessity, possibility, permission, or ability. They're essential for polite communication when traveling.

What Are Modal Verbs?

Modal verbs are helping verbs that express:

  • Ability (can, could)
  • Permission (may, can)
  • Obligation (must, should, have to)
  • Possibility (might, may, could)
  • Advice (should, ought to)

Modal verbs are followed by the base form of the main verb (without "to").

Example: "You should visit the castle." (Not "You should to visit")

Important Rules:

  • Modal verbs don't change form for different subjects (I/you/he/she/it/we/they)
  • They don't have infinitive or -ing forms
  • Questions are formed by inverting the modal verb and subject
  • Negatives are formed by adding "not" after the modal verb

Modal Verbs by Degrees of Certainty

High Certainty

Must

Strong obligation or logical conclusion

"You must have a passport to travel abroad."

"She's not answering. She must be busy."

Will

Strong prediction or promise

"The train will arrive at 3 PM."

"I will call you when I arrive."

Should

Strong recommendation

"You should try the local food."

"We should be there by noon."

Medium Certainty

Could

Possibility or ability in the past

"We could visit the museum tomorrow."

"I could speak some English when I was younger."

May

Possibility or permission (formal)

"It may rain later today."

"May I take your photo?"

Would

Polite requests or hypothetical situations

"Would you like something to drink?"

"I would visit Paris if I had more time."

Low Certainty

Might

Small possibility

"We might go to the beach if the weather improves."

"She might join us later."

Could

Small possibility

"The bus could be late due to traffic."

"This could be the right street."

May

Small possibility

"She may not understand English well."

"The museum may be closed today."

Modal Verbs in Travel Situations

Asking for Help

  • Could you help me with my luggage?
  • Would you mind showing me the way?
  • Can you recommend a good restaurant?
  • Could you tell me where the bathroom is?

Making Plans

  • We should visit the castle tomorrow.
  • We could go shopping in the afternoon.
  • We might have time for the museum.
  • You must try the local specialty.

Giving Advice

  • You should exchange money at the bank.
  • You shouldn't leave your passport in the hotel.
  • You might want to book tickets in advance.
  • You ought to try the local cuisine.

Expressing Possibility

  • The museum may be closed on Mondays.
  • The train could be delayed.
  • It might rain later today.
  • There should be a pharmacy nearby.

Speaking Practice

Practice your English speaking skills with these travel-related prompts and scenarios. Try to use modal verbs and the vocabulary you've learned.

Conversation Prompts

At the Airport

Imagine you're at the check-in counter. Practice this conversation:

Agent: Good morning. May I see your passport and ticket, please?

You: Good morning. Here you are.

Agent: Would you like a window or aisle seat?

You: _______________ (your response)

Agent: Do you have any luggage to check in?

You: _______________ (your response)

Try to use modal verbs like "would," "could," or "may" in your responses.

At the Hotel

Practice checking into a hotel:

Receptionist: Good afternoon. How can I help you?

You: _______________ (your response about having a reservation)

Receptionist: Yes, I see your reservation. You'll be staying with us for three nights, correct?

You: _______________ (confirm and ask about breakfast)

Receptionist: Yes, breakfast is included. It's served from 7 to 10 AM.

Practice using "could," "would," and "is/are there" in your questions.

Asking for Directions

You're lost and need to find your way to a famous landmark:

You: _______________ (ask how to get to a landmark)

Local: You can take the metro to Central Station, then walk about 10 minutes.

You: _______________ (ask how often the metro runs)

Local: It runs every 5 minutes during the day.

You: _______________ (thank them and ask if there's anything worth seeing nearby)

Use "could," "would," and "should" in your questions.

At a Restaurant

Practice ordering food at a restaurant:

Waiter: Are you ready to order?

You: _______________ (ask for recommendations)

Waiter: Our fish of the day is excellent, and many people enjoy our pasta dishes.

You: _______________ (order something and ask about ingredients)

Waiter: Would you like anything to drink with that?

You: _______________ (your response)

Practice using "could," "would," and "may" for polite requests.
