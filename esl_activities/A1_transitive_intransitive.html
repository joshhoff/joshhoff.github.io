Master Transitive & Intransitive Verbs

📚 Learn the Basics

Transitive Verbs

A transitive verb requires a direct object to complete its meaning. It transfers action from the subject to an object.

Example: She bought a book.
(What did she buy? → a book)
Example: The chef prepared dinner.
(What did the chef prepare? → dinner)
Example: They wrote a letter.
(What did they write? → a letter)
Example: Maria loves chocolate.
(What does Maria love? → chocolate)

Common Transitive Verbs: bring, carry, eat, find, give, hold, make, open, read, send, throw, watch, want, build, create, describe, enjoy, fix, grab, help, invite, kick, leave, meet, need, offer, play, question

Intransitive Verbs

An intransitive verb does not require a direct object. The action is complete by itself.

Example: The baby cried.
(No object needed)
Example: Birds fly.
(No object needed)
Example: The sun rises every morning.
(No object needed)
Example: We waited patiently.
(No object needed)

Common Intransitive Verbs: appear, arrive, dance, die, exist, fall, go, happen, laugh, listen, live, occur, sleep, smile, sneeze, swim, talk, travel, wait, walk, work, yawn, sit, stand, jump, run, respond

⚠️ Tricky Verbs - Can Be Both!

Some verbs can be transitive OR intransitive depending on the context:

RUN:
Intransitive: "She runs every day." (no object)
Transitive: "She runs a business." (runs what? → a business)
SING:
Intransitive: "The birds sing beautifully." (no object)
Transitive: "She sang a song." (sang what? → a song)
GROW:
Intransitive: "Children grow quickly." (no object)
Transitive: "Farmers grow vegetables." (grow what? → vegetables)

Other Dual-Purpose Verbs: move, change, begin, start, improve, increase, break, burn, drive, study, paint

💬 Conversation Prompts

🗣️ Discussion Topic 1

Prompt: Describe your morning routine using at least 3 transitive verbs and 2 intransitive verbs. Can you identify which is which?

Example starter: "I woke up (intransitive), made breakfast (transitive)..."

🗣️ Discussion Topic 2

Prompt: Tell a story about a memorable day. After telling the story, go back and identify all the transitive verbs. What objects do they act upon?

Prompt: Some verbs can be both transitive and intransitive depending on context. Can you think of examples? Try using the verb "run" in two different ways.

Hint: "She runs every morning" vs "She runs a business"
