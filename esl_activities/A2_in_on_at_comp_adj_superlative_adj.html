Prepositions of Place: in / on / at

Explanation

IN

Use in for enclosed spaces and larger areas.

  • in a room
  • in London
  • in a box
  • in the garden
  • in the morning
  • in summer

ON

Use on for surfaces and specific days.

  • on the table
  • on the wall
  • on the floor
  • on the bus
  • on Monday
  • on Christmas Day

AT

Use at for specific points and times.

  • at the bus stop
  • at the door
  • at the top
  • at 3 o'clock
  • at night
  • at lunchtime

3. Conversation Practice:

Choose the correct preposition and explain why:

Situation 1: Describing where you live

Complete: "I live _____ a small apartment _____ the third floor _____ the city center."

Answer: "I live in a small apartment on the third floor in the city center."

Explanation:

  • in a small apartment - apartments are enclosed spaces
  • on the third floor - floors are surfaces/levels
  • in the city center - city center is a larger area

Situation 2: Meeting a friend

Complete: "Let's meet _____ the café _____ Main Street _____ 5 o'clock."

Answer: "Let's meet at the café on Main Street at 5 o'clock."

Explanation:

  • at the café - specific location/point
  • on Main Street - streets are treated as surfaces
  • at 5 o'clock - specific time

Situation 3: Describing a photo

Complete: "_____ this photo, my family is sitting _____ a bench _____ the park."

Answer: "In this photo, my family is sitting on a bench in the park."

Explanation:

  • In this photo - photos are treated as containers
  • on a bench - benches are surfaces
  • in the park - parks are larger areas

Comparative Adjectives

Explanation

We use comparative adjectives to compare two things and show differences between them.

One-syllable adjectives

Add -er to the adjective

  • tall → taller
  • small → smaller
  • cheap → cheaper
  • fast → faster

Note: For adjectives ending in e, just add -r (nice → nicer)

Two-syllable (and longer)

Use more + adjective

  • careful → more careful
  • expensive → more expensive
  • interesting → more interesting
  • beautiful → more beautiful

Note: Some two-syllable adjectives can take -er (e.g., clever → cleverer)

Irregular forms

Some adjectives have special comparative forms

  • good → better
  • bad → worse
  • far → farther/further
  • little → less
  • many/much → more

Spelling Rules

  • For adjectives ending in consonant + single vowel + consonant, double the final consonant:
    big → bigger, hot → hotter
  • For adjectives ending in y, change y to i and add -er:
    happy → happier, easy → easier

Superlative Adjectives

Explanation

We use superlative adjectives to compare one thing with all others in the same group. Superlatives show the highest degree of a quality among three or more items.

One-syllable adjectives

Add the + -est to the adjective

  • tall → the tallest
  • small → the smallest
  • cheap → the cheapest
  • fast → the fastest

Note: For adjectives ending in e, just add -st (nice → the nicest)

Two-syllable (and longer)

Use the most + adjective

  • careful → the most careful
  • expensive → the most expensive
  • interesting → the most interesting
  • beautiful → the most beautiful

Note: Some two-syllable adjectives can take -est (e.g., clever → the cleverest)

Irregular forms

Some adjectives have special superlative forms

  • good → the best
  • bad → the worst
  • far → the farthest/furthest
  • little → the least
  • many/much → the most

Spelling Rules

  • For adjectives ending in consonant + single vowel + consonant, double the final consonant:
    big → the biggest, hot → the hottest
  • For adjectives ending in y, change y to i and add -est:
    happy → the happiest, easy → the easiest

2. Conversation Practice:

Answer these questions using superlative adjectives in your responses.

1. What is the best trip you have ever taken?

Example answer: The best trip I've ever taken was to Japan. It was the most interesting country I've visited because it has the most unique culture. The food was the tastiest I've ever had, and the people were the friendliest.

2. Who is the most important person in your life?

Example answer: My grandmother is the most important person in my life. She is the wisest person I know and gives me the best advice. She's also the most supportive when I face the hardest challenges.

3. What is the most difficult thing about learning English?

Example answer: For me, pronunciation is the most difficult thing about learning English. Irregular verbs are the most confusing, and phrasal verbs are the hardest to remember. However, grammar is becoming the easiest part with practice.
