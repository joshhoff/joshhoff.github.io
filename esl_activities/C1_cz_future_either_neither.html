Either vs Neither

EITHER

Used for positive choices between two options

Either coffee or tea is fine.

(Czech: Buď káva nebo čaj je v pořádku.)

You can take either bus.

(Czech: Můžeš jet kterýmkoliv autobusem.)

NEITHER

Used for negative statements about two options

Neither option works for me.

(Czech: Ani jedna možnost mi nevyhovuje.)

Neither John nor Mary came.

(Czech: Ani John ani Mary nepřišli.)

Key Rules:

  • • Either...or = one of two positive choices
  • • Neither...nor = none of two options (negative)
  • • Don't use 'not' with 'neither' (it's already negative)

Future Tense in English

WILL + Verb

Spontaneous decisions, predictions, promises

I will help you.

(Spontaneous offer)

It will rain tomorrow.

(Prediction)

GOING TO + Verb

Planned actions, intentions, obvious future

I'm going to study tonight.

(Planned intention)

Look! It's going to rain.

(Evidence-based)

Present Continuous

Fixed arrangements, scheduled events

I'm meeting John at 3pm.

(Fixed arrangement)

We're flying to Prague tomorrow.

(Scheduled)

When to Use Each Form:

WILL
  • • Instant decisions
  • • Predictions without evidence
  • • Promises and offers
GOING TO
  • • Pre-planned intentions
  • • Predictions with evidence
  • • Obvious future events
PRESENT CONTINUOUS
  • • Fixed appointments
  • • Definite arrangements
  • • Scheduled events
