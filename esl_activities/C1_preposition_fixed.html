Advanced English for C1 Learners

Why Does English Have Fixed Prepositions?

Unlike many languages where preposition choice follows transparent semantic logic, English frequently pairs verbs, adjectives, and nouns with arbitrary, lexically-determined prepositions. These collocations are the residue of historical forces:

1. Germanic & Latin Layering

English inherited phrasal patterns from Old English (Germanic) and then absorbed thousands of Latin/French constructions after 1066. Many fixed prepositions preserve the original language's logic even though that logic is invisible in modern English. "Depend on" echoes French dépendre de; "insist on" carries forward Latin insistere + ablative.

2. Metaphorical Extension

Spatial prepositions were repurposed for abstract meanings. "Keen on" originally described physical orientation; "prone to" started as a bodily posture. Once metaphors crystallized, the preposition became fixed regardless of whether the spatial meaning remains obvious.

3. Analogy & Frequency

High-frequency pairings become entrenched through repetition. Speakers generalize patterns—if "interested in" and "engaged in" work, learners might expect *"enthusiastic in," but convention settled on "enthusiastic about." These are arbitrary conventions maintained by communal usage.

4. No Governing Rule

Unlike case-governed prepositions in German or Russian, English provides no morphological signal. The preposition must be memorized as part of the lexical entry—treat "apologize for" as a single vocabulary item, not two separate words.

💡 Learning Strategy for C1

Record fixed prepositions in collocational chunks. When you encounter "abstain from," store the whole phrase—not just "abstain." Use spaced repetition and contextual sentences rather than isolated word lists.

Common C1 Fixed Preposition Categories

Verb + Preposition

  • account for • refrain from
  • comply with • resort to
  • dispose of • embark on
  • abstain from • abide by

Adjective + Preposition

  • conducive to • averse to
  • susceptible to • immune to
  • contingent on • intent on
  • indicative of • reminiscent of

Noun + Preposition

  • aptitude for • disregard for
  • reliance on • emphasis on
  • aversion to • objection to
  • discrepancy between

Tricky Pairs

  • conform to vs. comply with
  • consist of vs. comprise (no prep)
  • prevent from vs. prohibit from
  • compare to vs. compare with
