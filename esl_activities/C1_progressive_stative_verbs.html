Present Progressive Tense

Master continuous actions with interactive learning

What is the Present Progressive?

Formation

The present progressive tense describes actions happening right now or temporary situations.

Formula: Subject + am/is/are + verb + -ing

✓ Correct Examples

  • • I am looking up new vocabulary words.
  • • She is working out at the gym right now.
  • • They are setting up the presentation.
  • • We are looking into the problem.

⚠️ Common Uses

  • • Actions happening now
  • • Temporary situations
  • • Future plans (with time expressions)
  • • Changing situations

Stative Verbs & Present Progressive

Important Rule

Stative verbs usually don't use the present progressive because they describe states, not actions. However, some can turn into action verbs in certain contexts.

✗ Usually NOT Progressive

  • • I know the answer. (not "am knowing")
  • • She loves chocolate. (not "is loving")
  • • They own a car. (not "are owning")
  • • We believe in hard work. (not "are believing")

These students often mess up with stative verbs!

✓ Sometimes Progressive

  • • I am thinking about the problem. (mental process)
  • • She is being very kind today. (temporary behavior)
  • • They are having dinner. (activity)
  • • We are seeing the doctor tomorrow. (meeting)

Context helps you figure out the meaning!

Two Verbs Together: Stative + Action

When sentences have both stative and action verbs, only the action verb can be progressive. This concept often trips up advanced learners!

✓ Correct Examples

  • • I know she is working late tonight.
    ↳ stative + progressive action
  • • They believe we are making progress.
    ↳ stative + progressive action
  • • She wants to carry on studying.
    ↳ stative + infinitive action
  • • We understand you are trying your best.
    ↳ stative + progressive action
  • • He loves hanging out with friends.
    ↳ stative + gerund action

✗ Common Mistakes

  • • I am knowing she is working late.
    ↳ Don't make stative verbs progressive!
  • • They are believing we make progress.
    ↳ "Believe" is stative, "make" should be progressive
  • • She is wanting to carry on studying.
    ↳ "Want" is usually stative
  • • We are understanding you try your best.
    ↳ Both verbs are wrong here!

💡 Pro Tip

When you come across sentences with multiple verbs, ask yourself: "Which verb describes an action happening now?" Only that verb gets the -ing form!

Common Stative Verbs

Mental States:
  • know, understand
  • believe, think
  • remember, forget
Emotions:
  • love, hate, like
  • want, need, prefer
  • hope, wish
Senses:
  • see, hear, smell
  • taste, feel
  • sound, look
Possession:
  • have, own, possess
  • belong, contain
  • include, consist

Speaking Practice

Practice speaking with these conversation prompts. Don't worry if you slip up - speaking practice helps you become more confident!

🏠 Daily Life & Current Activities

Conversation Starters

What are you doing right now?

Sample responses:

  • • "I'm going over my notes for tomorrow's exam."
  • • "I'm listening to music while I sort out my room."
  • • "I'm thinking about what to cook for dinner."

Your turn: Describe what you're actually doing right now!

How is your day going?

Sample responses:

  • • "It's going well! I'm working on a project I really enjoy."
  • • "I'm having a busy day, but I love what I'm doing."
  • • "I'm feeling tired because I'm dealing with a lot of tasks."

Practice: Mix stative verbs (feel, love) with progressive actions!

What's your family doing these days?

Sample responses:

  • • "My sister is getting ready for her wedding next month."
  • • "My parents are thinking about moving to a smaller house."
  • • "We're all looking forward to our vacation."

Challenge: Use both present progressive and stative verbs in your answer!

Role-Play Scenarios

📞 Phone Call with a Friend

Call a friend who's studying abroad. Ask what they're doing, how they're feeling, and what they're learning.

Focus: Use "What are you doing?" "How are you feeling?" "Are you enjoying...?"
🏢 Office Small Talk

You meet a colleague in the elevator. Make small talk about current projects and weekend plans.

Focus: "I'm working on..." "I'm planning to..." "I think..."
🎓 Study Group Discussion

Discuss what everyone is studying and how they're preparing for exams.

Focus: "I'm focusing on..." "I understand..." "We're reviewing..."

💼 Work & Study Situations

Professional Conversations

Tell me about your current project.

Professional responses:

  • • "I'm focusing on improving our customer service system."
  • • "We're developing a new app that I believe will help students."
  • • "I'm working with the design team on this."

Practice tip: Combine what you're doing with what you think/believe about it!

How do you feel about your workload?

Honest responses:

  • • "I feel challenged, but I'm keeping up with everything."
  • • "I love what I'm doing, even though I'm working long hours."
  • • "I'm aiming for better work-life balance."

Remember: Feelings (love, feel) are stative, actions (working, aiming) are progressive!

Academic Discussions

What are you studying this semester?

Academic responses:

  • • "I'm taking advanced mathematics and I'm really enjoying it."
  • • "I'm focusing on literature because I want to become a teacher."
  • • "I'm finding chemistry challenging, but I understand the concepts better now."

Mix it up: What you're doing + how you feel about it!

How are you preparing for exams?

Study strategies:

  • • "I'm making flashcards and I think they're helping me remember better."
  • • "I'm joining study groups because I learn better with others."
  • • "I'm reviewing past exams and I understand the patterns now."

Challenge: Explain your method AND your opinion about its effectiveness!

🔮 Future Plans & Personal Opinions

Future & Ongoing Plans

What are you planning for next year?

Future-focused responses:

  • • "I'm thinking about changing careers because I want more flexibility."
  • • "I'm planning to travel more, and I'm saving up money for it."
  • • "I'm looking into graduate programs that I believe will help my career."

Pattern: Present progressive for plans + stative verbs for desires/beliefs!

What changes are happening in your life?

Life transition responses:

  • • "I'm getting used to living alone, and I love the independence."
  • • "My family is moving to a new city, but I prefer staying here."
  • • "I'm building up my confidence in public speaking."

Advanced tip: Describe the change (progressive) and your feelings about it (stative)!

Opinions & Preferences

What do you think about remote work?

Opinion-based responses:

  • • "I believe it's great, and I'm getting better at managing my time."
  • • "I prefer working from home, but I'm missing the social interaction."
  • • "I think it depends on the person. I'm finding out what works for me."

Structure: State your opinion + describe your current experience!

How do you feel about social media?

Balanced responses:

  • • "I understand its benefits, but I'm cutting back on my usage."
  • • "I love staying connected, though I'm watching out for misinformation."
  • • "I think it's useful for business, and I'm learning how to use it professionally."

Advanced: Express complex thoughts using both verb types!

💡 Speaking Tips & Practice Strategies

🎯 Before Speaking

  • • Think: "What am I doing?" vs "What do I think?"
  • • Plan to mix actions with opinions
  • • Don't worry about perfect grammar
  • • Focus on communication first

🗣️ While Speaking

  • • Use "I'm..." for current actions
  • • Use "I think/feel/believe..." for opinions
  • • Take your time to think
  • • Self-correct if you notice mistakes

📝 After Speaking

  • • Notice which verbs you used
  • • Did you mix stative and progressive?
  • • Think about what felt natural
  • • Practice the difficult parts again
