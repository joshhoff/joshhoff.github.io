📚 Passive Voice Mastery

Learn and practice the passive voice in English

What is the Passive Voice?

The passive voice is used when we want to focus on the action or the person/thing receiving the action, rather than who performs the action.

Compare Active vs Passive:

Active: The chef cooks the meal.
(Focus on who does the action)
Passive: The meal is cooked by the chef.
(Focus on what receives the action)

How to Form the Passive Voice

Subject + BE + Past Participle + (by + agent)

The form of "BE" changes based on tense and subject

Examples in Different Tenses:

Present Simple: They build houses.
Present Simple: Houses are built (by them).
Past Simple: She wrote the letter.
Past Simple: The letter was written (by her).
Present Perfect: We have finished the project.
Present Perfect: The project has been finished (by us).

When to Use Passive Voice

  • Unknown agent: "My bike was stolen." (We don't know who did it)
  • Obvious agent: "The criminal was arrested." (Obviously by police)
  • Focus on action: "The bridge was built in 1995." (The bridge is more important than who built it)
  • Formal writing: "Mistakes were made." (More formal than "We made mistakes")

Practice Exercises

Convert these active sentences to passive voice:

Convert: "The teacher explains the lesson."
The lesson explains by the teacher.
The lesson is explained by the teacher.
The lesson was explained by the teacher.
Convert: "They will complete the project tomorrow."
The project will complete tomorrow.
The project will be completed tomorrow.
The project is completed tomorrow.
Convert: "Someone has stolen my wallet."
My wallet has stolen.
My wallet has been stolen.
My wallet was stolen.
