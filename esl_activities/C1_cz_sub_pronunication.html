🎭 Subjunctive Mood Practice
Scenario: You're in a job interview discussing hypothetical situations and company policies.
Practice these subjunctive forms:
• "If I were the manager..." (contrary to fact)
• "I suggest that he be promoted..." (formal suggestion)
• "It's important that she arrive on time..." (necessity)
Czech Note: Unlike Czech conditional mood (kondicionál), English subjunctive often uses base verb forms. Czech: "Kdyby byl manažerem..." vs English: "If he were manager..."
Role-play: You're giving advice to a friend about their relationship problems.
Use these subjunctive expressions:
• "If I were you, I would..."
• "I wish you were more confident..."
• "It's crucial that you be honest with her..."
Business Meeting: Discuss company policies and make formal recommendations.
Formal subjunctive patterns:
• "I recommend that the policy be reviewed..."
• "It's essential that everyone attend the meeting..."
• "I propose that we postpone the decision..."
🔄 Advanced Phrasal Verbs
CLAMP DOWN ON Advanced

Meaning: To take strict action to stop something

Czech equivalent: "zakročit proti", "tvrdě zasáhnout"
Conversation starter: "The government needs to clamp down on tax evasion. What's your opinion on stricter financial regulations?"
PHASE OUT Advanced

Meaning: To gradually stop using or providing something

Czech equivalent: "postupně zrušit", "vyřadit z provozu"
Conversation starter: "Many companies are phasing out remote work policies. How do you feel about returning to the office?"
COTTON ON TO Advanced

Meaning: To begin to understand or realize something

Czech equivalent: "přijít na to", "pochopit"
Conversation starter: "It took me ages to cotton on to what she was really trying to say. Have you ever misunderstood someone's hints?"
BRUSH UP ON Intermediate

Meaning: To improve or refresh your knowledge of something

Czech equivalent: "oprášit si znalosti", "zdokonalit se"
Conversation starter: "I need to brush up on my presentation skills before the conference. What skills would you like to improve?"
🗣️ Pronunciation Challenges for Czech Speakers
TH Sounds (θ/ð) - Most challenging for Czech speakers
THINK /θɪŋk/
THIS /ðɪs/
THROUGH /θruː/
ALTHOUGH /ɔːlˈðoʊ/
Czech speakers often substitute /s/ or /z/ for TH sounds. Practice: tongue between teeth!
W vs V Distinction
WINE /waɪn/
VINE /vaɪn/
WEST /west/
VEST /vest/
Czech has only /v/ sound. For /w/: round lips, no teeth contact!
Final Consonant Clusters
ASKED /æskt/
MONTHS /mʌnθs/
STRENGTHS /streŋθs/
TWELFTHS /twelfθs/
Czech speakers often add vowels between consonants. Practice saying all consonants clearly!
