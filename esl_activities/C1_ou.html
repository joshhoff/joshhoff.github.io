Introduction to the "ou" Sound

Welcome to our exploration of the English "ou" sound pattern! This diphthong (two vowel sounds combined) appears in many common English words when "ou" is followed by a consonant. It's pronounced as /aʊ/ in the International Phonetic Alphabet, starting with an "ah" sound and gliding to an "oo" sound.

Phonetic Breakdown:

The "ou" sound is represented as /aʊ/ in IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet).

How to Form the "ou" Sound

Mouth Position & Formation

To pronounce the "ou" sound correctly:

  1. Start with your mouth open in an "ah" position
  2. As you make the sound, gradually close your lips into a rounded "oo" shape
  3. The sound glides from one position to the other - it's a diphthong (two vowel sounds combined)
  4. Your tongue starts low and moves upward toward the back of your mouth

Try it yourself:

Say "ah" then quickly transition to "oo" without stopping: "ah-oo" → /aʊ/

Spelling Patterns

Common Patterns:
  • "ou" followed by consonants: sound, found, mouth
  • "ow" at the end of words: now, how, cow
  • "ou" in the middle of words: house, mouse, loud
Exceptions:
  • "ou" can also make other sounds:
  • As in "though" /əʊ/
  • As in "cough" /ɒf/
  • As in "through" /uː/
