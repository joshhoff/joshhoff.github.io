📖 Modal Verbs in Challenging Contexts

1. Degrees of Certainty

High Certainty

Must: "She must be at home" (logical deduction)

Will: "He will arrive soon" (prediction)

Medium Certainty

Should/Ought to: "It should work" (expectation)

May/Might: "She might call" (possibility)

2. Professional Contexts

Could: Polite suggestions - "We could consider alternative approaches."

Would: Hypothetical situations - "I would recommend consulting an expert."

Shall: Formal offers/suggestions - "Shall we proceed with the proposal?"

3. Past Modals (Advanced)

Must have: Past deduction - "He must have forgotten the meeting."

Could have: Past possibility - "She could have taken a different route."

Should have: Past advice/regret - "You should have called earlier."

Exercise 1: Choose the Best Modal

2. You _____ have informed the client about the delay earlier.

📝 Written Homework Assignment

Assignment Instructions

Complete the following writing tasks using appropriate modal verbs. Focus on accuracy and natural usage in context.

⏰ Time Limit: 45 minutes

📊 Target: Use at least 8 different modal verbs

Task 1: Professional Email (150-200 words)

Write an email to your supervisor about a project delay. Use modal verbs to:

  • Express possibility about completion dates
  • Make polite suggestions for solutions
  • Show regret about the situation
  • Discuss what could have been done differently
Word count: 0/200

Task 2: Advice Column Response (100-150 words)

Someone wrote: "I'm 25 and still living with my parents. I have a job but I'm scared to move out. What should I do?"

Write a response using modal verbs to give advice, express possibilities, and discuss hypothetical situations.

Word count: 0/150

Task 3: Modal Verb Analysis (50-75 words)

Analyze this sentence and rewrite it using different modal verbs to change the meaning:

"You must submit the report by Friday."

Provide 3 alternative versions with different modal verbs and explain how the meaning changes.

Word count: 0/75
