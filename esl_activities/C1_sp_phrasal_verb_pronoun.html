Master Pronoun Placement in Phrasal Verbs

What are Phrasal Verbs?

Phrasal verbs are combinations of a verb and a particle (usually a preposition or adverb) that create a meaning different from the original verb.

Examples:

  • Turn on the light. (= activate)
  • Look after the children. (= take care of)
  • Give up smoking. (= quit)

The Pronoun Challenge

When we replace a noun with a pronoun in a phrasal verb sentence, we need to know where to place the pronoun.

Original

Turn on the light.

→

With Pronoun

Turn it on.

Types of Phrasal Verbs

Separable Phrasal Verbs

The pronoun must go between the verb and the particle.

Example:

Turn off the light. → Turn it off.

❌ Turn off it.

Inseparable Phrasal Verbs

The pronoun must go after the particle.

Example:

Look after the children. → Look after them.

❌ Look them after.

Understanding Pronoun Placement

The Pronoun Test

To determine if a phrasal verb is separable or inseparable, try replacing the object with a pronoun:

If you can put the pronoun between the verb and particle:

Pick up the book. → Pick it up. ✓

→ The phrasal verb is SEPARABLE

If you must put the pronoun after the particle:

Look after the baby. → Look after him. ✓

→ The phrasal verb is INSEPARABLE

Common Phrasal Verbs

Separable Phrasal Verbs

  • Turn on/off - Turn it on/off
  • Pick up - Pick it up
  • Throw away - Throw it away
  • Give back - Give it back
  • Fill out - Fill it out
  • Put on - Put it on
  • Take off - Take it off
  • Write down - Write it down

Inseparable Phrasal Verbs

  • Look after - Look after them
  • Get over - Get over it
  • Run into - Run into her
  • Look for - Look for it
  • Wait for - Wait for him
  • Agree with - Agree with them
  • Believe in - Believe in it
  • Deal with - Deal with it

Special Cases

Some phrasal verbs can be both separable and inseparable depending on context:

  • When the object is a noun, you can often choose: "Turn on the light" or "Turn the light on"
  • When the object is a pronoun, you must follow the rules above

Conversation Prompts

Practice with a partner using these prompts:

1. Ask your partner to turn something on or off using a pronoun.

Example: "Could you turn it on, please?"

2. Tell a story using at least 3 different phrasal verbs with pronouns.

Example: "I picked it up, turned it on, and then gave it back to her."

3. Ask your partner to look for something and use a pronoun in the response.

Example: "I can't find my keys." / "I'll help you look for them."
