English for Spanish Speakers

Take · Bring · Grab

Master three essential English verbs and stop mixing them up with Spanish "coger," "llevar," and "traer"

🫳

To Grab

The action of taking something with your hand

"Grab" means to quickly take or hold something in your hand. It's similar to Spanish coger in Spain. It focuses on the action of taking, not the direction. You grab something that's already near you—it's immediate and physical.

🇬🇧 English

"Grab the keys from the table."

🇪🇸 Spanish

"Coge las llaves de la mesa."

🇬🇧 English

"Grab my hand and hold it."

🇪🇸 Spanish

"Coge mi mano y sostenla."

💡 Key Insight

"Grab" is the action of holding or taking something. No movement involved—just the physical grip. Think: Spanish "coger" when you're not moving the object anywhere.

🚶‍♂️

To Take

Moving something from here to another place

"Take" means to carry or move something away from where you are. In Spanish, this is llevar. The key difference from "grab" is that with "take," something is moving somewhere else. You're not just holding it—you're transporting it. This is also used when you escort someone somewhere, or when you're wearing clothing (carrying it on your body).

🇬🇧 English

"Take this package to the post office."

🇪🇸 Spanish

"Lleva este paquete al correo."

🇬🇧 English

"She is taking her children to school."

🇪🇸 Spanish

"Ella lleva a sus hijos a la escuela."

💡 Key Insight

"Take" has direction built in—something moves from your location to somewhere else. This is why Spanish uses llevar. Think of it as the opposite of "bring."

📦

To Bring

Moving something toward where you are (or toward a reference point)

"Bring" means to carry or move something toward you or toward a destination. In Spanish, this is traer. It's the opposite direction from "take." Someone brings something to you; you take something away from them. "Bring" is always directional and clear—it points toward the listener or a location.

🇬🇧 English

"Bring me a glass of water."

🇪🇸 Spanish

"Traeme un vaso de agua."

🇬🇧 English

"She brought flowers to the party."

🇪🇸 Spanish

"Ella trajo flores a la fiesta."

💡 Key Insight

"Bring" points toward you or toward the destination. The classic pair: "take" = away from you ↔ "bring" = toward you.

🔄

The Spanish Connection: Why These Words Confuse You

You already understand these concepts in Spanish through coger, llevar, and traer. English organizes these ideas slightly differently. Here's how the concepts map:

🇪🇸 Spanish "Coger"

This is the action of taking something—just grabbing it with no direction involved.

  • "Coge el lápiz" → "Grab the pencil" (just the action)
  • "Cojo el autobús" → "I catch the bus" (taking/boarding, Spain)
  • "Coge mi mano" → "Grab my hand" (hold it, no motion)

🇬🇧 English "Grab"

This word captures that exact meaning—the action of taking something quickly.

  • "Grab the pencil" ✓ (quick action, same as "coge")
  • "I grab the bus" ✗ (sounds odd in English; we say "catch")
  • "Grab my hand" ✓ (hold it, same as "coge")

💡 The Core Pattern: LLEVAR = English "Take" in Most Real Situations

English "take" is ambiguous. Spanish "llevar" is not.

Most native "take" sentences are actually about moving something away. English lumps this under "take," but Spanish requires the directional verb llevar:

  • 📦 "Take this package to the post office" → Lleva este paquete al correo
  • 🚗 "Take the car to the repair shop" → Lleva el coche al taller
  • 👕 "Take your laundry to the cleaners" → Lleva tu ropa a la lavandería
  • 🎂 "Take the cake to grandma's house" → Lleva el pastel a casa de la abuela

🇪🇸 Spanish "Traer"

Direction toward you—bringing something from somewhere to your location.

  • "¿Me traes agua?" → "Will you bring me water?"
  • "Ella trajo flores" → "She brought flowers" (to us/the event)
  • "Trae tu libro a clase" → "Bring your book to class"

🇬🇧 English "Bring"

Direction toward you—moving something toward your location or a destination.

  • "Will you bring me water?" ✓ (same as "traer")
  • "She brought flowers" ✓ (toward us, same as "traer")
  • "Bring your book to class" ✓ (toward the class)

💡 The Real Difference: Word Order

In Spanish, you can feel the direction in the words themselves. In English, you feel it more through word order and context:

  • Spanish uses clear verbs: "Llevar" = away, "Traer" = toward
  • English is trickier: "Take" and "bring" rely on the whole sentence to show direction
  • "Take to X" = carrying away to destination (like "llevar")
  • "Bring to X" = carrying toward destination (like "traer")

🇪🇸 Spanish "Llevar"

Direction away from you—carrying something from here to another place.

  • "Lleva esto al correo" → "Take this to the post office"
  • "Ella lleva a los niños a la escuela" → "She takes the kids to school"
  • "¿Qué llevas puesto?" → "What are you wearing?" (carrying on your body)

🇬🇧 English "Take"

Direction away from you—moving something from your location somewhere else.

  • "Take this to the post office" ✓ (same as "llevar")
  • "She takes the kids to school" ✓ (same as "llevar")
  • "Take this book home" ✓ (moving away from here)
