Guía de Verbos

Coger · Llevar · Traer

Master three essential Spanish verbs and their nuanced English translations

🫳

Coger

To take / To grab / To catch / To pick up

In Spain, coger is an everyday verb meaning to take, grab, or catch something. It's used constantly — catching a bus, picking up an object, or taking something from a shelf. ⚠️ Important: In many Latin American countries, "coger" has a vulgar sexual connotation. Use tomar or agarrar instead in those regions.

🇪🇸 Spanish

"Voy a coger el autobús."

🇬🇧 English

"I'm going to catch/take the bus."

🇪🇸 Spanish

"Coge esa manzana de la mesa."

🇬🇧 English

"Grab/Pick up that apple from the table."

💡 Key Insight

English uses different verbs (take, grab, catch, pick up) depending on context, but in Spain they all map to coger.

🚶‍♂️

Llevar

To take (away) / To carry / To bring (away from here) / To wear

Llevar implies movement away from the current location — carrying or taking something to another place. It also means "to wear" clothing and "to have been" doing something (time expressions).

🇪🇸 Spanish

"Voy a llevar estos libros a la biblioteca."

🇬🇧 English

"I'm going to take these books to the library."

🇪🇸 Spanish

"Ella lleva un vestido rojo."

🇬🇧 English

"She is wearing a red dress."

💡 Key Insight

Think of llevar as pointing ➡️ away. You're moving something from here to there. The "wearing" meaning comes from "carrying clothes on your body."

📦

Traer

To bring (toward here) / To carry (to this place)

Traer implies movement toward the speaker's location. It's the opposite direction of llevar. Someone brings something to where you are.

🇪🇸 Spanish

"¿Puedes traerme un vaso de agua?"

🇬🇧 English

"Can you bring me a glass of water?"

🇪🇸 Spanish

"Ella trajo flores a la fiesta."

🇬🇧 English

"She brought flowers to the party."

💡 Key Insight

Think of traer as pointing ⬅️ toward you. The classic pair: llevar = take away, traer = bring here.
