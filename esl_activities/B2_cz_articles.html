Understanding English Articles for Czech Speakers

Czech doesn't have articles, so this concept is completely new! Think of articles as "word helpers" that give extra information about nouns.

📖 "A" and "An" (Indefinite Articles)

Use with: Singular countable nouns when talking about "one of many" or "any example"

"A" before consonant sounds: a book, a university, a European

"An" before vowel sounds: an apple, an hour, an honest person

Czech equivalent: Think like "jeden/jedna/jedno" but much more common in English

🎯 "The" (Definite Article)

Use when: Both speaker and listener know which specific thing you mean

  • Unique things: the sun, the President
  • Previously mentioned: I saw a dog. The dog was big.
  • Superlatives: the best, the tallest
  • Specific groups: the Czech Republic, the elderly
Czech equivalent: Similar to "ten/ta/to" but used much more frequently

🚫 No Article (Zero Article)

Use with:

  • Plural countable nouns (general): Dogs are friendly
  • Uncountable nouns (general): Water is essential
  • Abstract concepts: Love is important
  • Most proper names: Prague, Czech Republic
  • Academic subjects: mathematics, history
This is most similar to Czech - no extra words needed!

⚠️ Common Czech Speaker Mistakes

  • ❌ "I go to school" → ✅ "I go to school" (no article with institutions)
  • ❌ "The life is beautiful" → ✅ "Life is beautiful" (abstract concepts)
  • ❌ "I play the football" → ✅ "I play football" (sports)
  • ❌ "In the Czech Republic we speak Czech" → ✅ "In the Czech Republic we speak Czech" (languages)
