Understanding "About" vs "Of"

Use "ABOUT" for:

1. Topics and subjects - when discussing what something concerns

✓ We talked about the environment.
✓ She wrote a book about her travels.

2. Approximate numbers or time

✓ There were about 50 people at the party.
✓ I'll arrive at about 3 PM.
Use "OF" for:

1. Possession or belonging - showing what something is part of

✓ The color of the sky is beautiful.
✓ She's a friend of mine.

2. Material or composition - what something is made from

✓ A cup of coffee
✓ A piece of cake

3. Quantities and measurements

✓ A lot of people
✓ Most of the students

Common Expressions

With "ABOUT":
  • think about
  • worry about
  • care about
  • complain about
  • dream about
With "OF":
  • think of (opinion)
  • consist of
  • remind of
  • approve of
  • die of

Practice Activities

📝 Activity 1: Choose the Correct Preposition
1. What do you think ___ climate change?
2. The majority ___ students passed the exam.
3. She's always complaining ___ her job.
4. This table is made ___ solid wood.
5. There were ___ 200 guests at the wedding.
💬 Conversation Prompts

Practice with a partner or write your responses:

🗣️ Prompt 1: Tell me about a book or movie you recently enjoyed. What was it about? What did you think of the ending?
🗣️ Prompt 2: Describe your hometown. What's the population of your city? What do most of the people do for work? What do you love most about living there?
🗣️ Prompt 3: What are you worried about these days? Is there something you care deeply about that you'd like to change?
🗣️ Prompt 4: Think of a memorable meal you've had. What did it consist of? What do you remember most about that experience?

Phrasal Verbs = One Spanish Word

These English phrasal verbs translate to a single word in Spanish, making them easier to remember!

Give up
→ Rendirse
"Don't give up on your dreams!"
Find out
→ Descubrir
"I need to find out the truth."
Look for
→ Buscar
"I'm looking for my keys."
Put off
→ Posponer
"Let's put off the meeting until tomorrow."
Turn down
→ Rechazar
"She turned down the job offer."
Bring up
→ Mencionar
"Don't bring up that topic again."
Show up
→ Aparecer
"He didn't show up to the party."
Get over
→ Superar
"It took time to get over the loss."
Run into
→ Encontrar
"I ran into an old friend yesterday."
Take off
→ Despegar
"The plane will take off soon."
Break down
→ Descomponerse
"My car broke down on the highway."
Carry out
→ Realizar
"We need to carry out this plan carefully."
