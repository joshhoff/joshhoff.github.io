Understanding Prefixes & Suffixes

What are Prefixes?

Prefixes are word parts added to the beginning of a base word to change its meaning. They help create new words with related but distinct meanings.

Example:

re- + write = rewrite (to write again)
un- + happy = unhappy (not happy)
pre- + view = preview (to view before)

What are Suffixes?

Suffixes are word parts added to the end of a base word to change its meaning or its part of speech (e.g., from a verb to a noun, or from an adjective to an adverb).

Example:

teach + -er = teacher (person who teaches)
quick + -ly = quickly (in a quick manner)
happy + -ness = happiness (state of being happy)

Why They Matter

Understanding prefixes and suffixes helps you:

  • Decode unfamiliar words by recognizing their parts
  • Expand your vocabulary exponentially
  • Understand the nuances between related words
  • Improve your spelling
  • Express yourself more precisely in both speaking and writing

English vs. Portuguese: Prefixes & Suffixes

As a Portuguese speaker, you'll notice many similarities between English and Portuguese affixes, as both languages have Latin and Greek roots. Understanding these connections can help you learn English affixes more efficiently.

Similarities & Differences

English Portuguese Notes
re- (again)
rewrite, rebuild 		re- (novamente)
reescrever, reconstruir 		Similar form and meaning in both languages
pre- (before)
preview, prehistoric 		pré- (antes)
pré-visualização, pré-histórico 		Nearly identical, with accent mark in Portuguese
un- (not)
unhappy, unable 		in- (não)
infeliz, incapaz 		Different forms but similar function
-tion (state/action)
education, action 		-ção (estado/ação)
educação, ação 		Similar pronunciation and function
-ment (result/action)
development, payment 		-mento (resultado/ação)
desenvolvimento, pagamento 		Very similar form and identical function
-ly (in a manner)
quickly, carefully 		-mente (de maneira)
rapidamente, cuidadosamente 		Different forms but identical function
-er/-or (person who)
teacher, actor 		-or/-dor (pessoa que)
professor, ator 		Similar forms and identical function

Key Insight for Portuguese Speakers:

Many English words with Latin roots will have similar prefixes and suffixes to Portuguese. Look for these patterns to help expand your vocabulary more quickly. For example, if you know the Portuguese word "impossível," you can guess that the English equivalent is "impossible."

Speaking Activities & Prompts

1. Word Building Chain

In pairs or small groups, take turns adding prefixes or suffixes to create new words.

Example:

Student 1: "happy"
Student 2: "unhappy" (adding prefix un-)
Student 3: "unhappiness" (adding suffix -ness)
Student 4: "unhappily" (changing to -ly instead)

Try to continue as long as possible. When you can't add any more affixes, start with a new base word.

2. Prefix/Suffix Categories

In groups, choose a prefix or suffix and take turns naming words that use it. The person who can't think of a new word within 10 seconds is out.

Example with "-able":

Student 1: "comfortable"
Student 2: "reliable"
Student 3: "affordable"
Student 4: "available"

Try with different prefixes and suffixes. For an extra challenge, explain the meaning of each word you say.

3. Prefix/Suffix Story Creation

In pairs, create a short story using as many words with prefixes and suffixes as possible. Then share your story with the class.

Example beginning:

"The unhappy scientist was rethinking his unsuccessful experiment. He had been overworking endlessly, but his coworkers were unsupportive..."

Try to include at least 10 words with prefixes and suffixes. Underline or highlight them in your story.

4. Translation Challenge

With a partner, translate these Portuguese words to English, paying attention to the prefixes and suffixes:

Portuguese:

  • impossível
  • desconfortável
  • pré-histórico
  • felizmente
  • reavaliar

English:

  • impossible
  • uncomfortable
  • prehistoric
  • happily
  • reevaluate

Discuss the similarities and differences in the prefixes and suffixes between the two languages.

5. Debate with Affixes

Hold a debate on a simple topic, but you must use at least one word with a prefix or suffix in each sentence you speak.

Debate topics:

  • Is technology making us more connected or disconnected?
  • Should homework be mandatory or optional?
  • Are online classes more effective than traditional classes?

After the debate, count how many different prefixed and suffixed words each team used.
