Understanding Mexican Spanish Phrasal Verbs

Welcome to our comprehensive guide on Mexican Spanish phrasal verbs and their English equivalents. As a C1 level English learner, understanding these nuances will help you communicate more naturally and effectively.

What are phrasal verbs?

Phrasal verbs are combinations of verbs and prepositions or adverbs that create expressions with meanings different from the individual words. While English relies heavily on phrasal verbs, Spanish often uses single verbs with different prepositions.

English Phrasal Verbs

English combines verbs with particles to create new meanings:

  • look up (search for information)
  • give up (surrender)
  • run into (meet by chance)

Mexican Spanish Approach

Mexican Spanish often uses:

  • Single verbs (buscar instead of "look up")
  • Different preposition combinations
  • Reflexive verbs where English uses phrasal verbs

Key Differences to Master

  1. Preposition usage differs significantly between languages
  2. Mexican Spanish often uses reflexive verbs where English uses phrasal verbs
  3. Some English phrasal verbs translate to single words in Spanish
  4. Mexican Spanish has regional expressions that don't directly translate

Common Mexican Spanish vs. English Phrasal Verbs

# Mexican Spanish English Equivalent Example Usage
1 Pensar en Think about/of Estoy pensando en ti.
I'm thinking about you.
2 Acabar con Finish off/End up with Acabó con toda la comida.
He finished off all the food.
3 Contar con Count on/Rely on Puedes contar conmigo.
You can count on me.
4 Quedar con Meet up with Quedé con Juan a las 5.
I'm meeting up with Juan at 5.
5 Soñar con Dream about/of Soñé con viajar a México.
I dreamed about traveling to Mexico.
6 Darse cuenta de Realize/Notice Me di cuenta de mi error.
I realized my mistake.
7 Enterarse de Find out about Me enteré de las noticias.
I found out about the news.
8 Preocuparse por Worry about No te preocupes por eso.
Don't worry about that.
9 Fijarse en Notice/Pay attention to Fíjate en los detalles.
Pay attention to the details.
10 Depender de Depend on Todo depende del clima.
Everything depends on the weather.
11 Echar de menos Miss (someone/something) Te echo de menos.
I miss you.
12 Llevarse bien/mal con Get along with/not get along with Me llevo bien con mis vecinos.
I get along with my neighbors.
13 Hacerse cargo de Take care of/Take charge of Me haré cargo del proyecto.
I'll take charge of the project.
14 Pasar por Stop by/Pass through Pasaré por tu casa.
I'll stop by your house.
15 Acabarse Run out Se acabó el café.
We ran out of coffee.
16 Ponerse + adjective Get + adjective Me puse nervioso.
I got nervous.
17 Meterse en problemas Get into trouble Siempre se mete en problemas.
He always gets into trouble.
18 Apuntarse a Sign up for Me apunté a clases de baile.
I signed up for dance classes.
19 Quedarse con Keep/Hold onto Me quedé con tu libro.
I kept your book.
20 Salirse con la suya Get away with it Siempre se sale con la suya.
He always gets away with it.

Regional Mexican Expressions

Mexican Spanish also has unique regional expressions that may not directly translate to English phrasal verbs:

  • Echar porras - To cheer someone on
  • Dar el avión - To dismiss someone (lit. "give the airplane")
  • Caer gordo/a - To be annoying to someone

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Direct Translation Errors

  • Avoid translating "look for" as "mirar para" (correct: buscar)
  • Don't translate "wait for" as "esperar por" (correct: esperar)
  • Avoid "pensar sobre" for "think about" (correct: pensar en)

Preposition Confusion

  • Using "en" instead of "de" with certain verbs
  • Confusing "por" and "para" in phrasal constructions
  • Omitting prepositions when they're required
