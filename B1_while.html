English Grammar Guide

While vs During

Understanding the difference

The Key Rule

During is a preposition — it comes before a noun.

While is a conjunction — it comes before a clause (subject + verb).

During + noun

"I fell asleep during the movie."

While + clause

"I fell asleep while I was watching the movie."

During

preposition → followed by a noun / noun phrase

  • ➜"It rained during the night."
  • ➜"She didn't speak during the meeting."
  • ➜"We met some great people during our holiday."
💡 Tip: If you can replace the word with "in the course of," you probably need during.

While

conjunction → followed by subject + verb

  • ➜"It rained while we were sleeping."
  • ➜"She listened to music while she was cooking."
  • ➜"While I was reading, the phone rang."
💡 Tip: "While" often describes two actions happening at the same time.

Common Mistakes

❌ "During I was eating, the phone rang."

✅ "While I was eating, the phone rang."

→ "I was eating" is a clause (subject + verb), so use while.

❌ "He slept while the flight."

✅ "He slept during the flight."

→ "the flight" is a noun, so use during.
